The hissing sound from the cracked seal was soft, almost drowned out by the thunderous slamming of twenty-foot swells against the fiberglass hull. Thorne looked at the weeping fracture in the stainless steel. For three seconds, denial warred with terror across his pale face. "The pressure drop is minor... the cryogenic reservoir should compensate..." "Drop it, Thorne," Elena commanded, taking a cautious step back toward the coxswain's console. Her eyes were locked on the thin, chalky plume spiraling into the recirculated air of the cabin. "Kick it into the bilge trough and open the sea-cock valve. Vent it into the ocean." "No!" Thorne snarled, his voice cracking. He pulled the cylinder closer to his chest, his uninjured arm wrapped around the freezing metal like a vise. "You don't throw away a trillion dollars of intellectual property! If we reach the extraction perimeter, the mobile labs in Reykjavik can repressurize it within hours!" The filaments didn't wait for Reykjavik. Drawn by the intense heat radiating from the open fracture of Thorne’s collarbone, the white tendrils surged forward. They didn't move like plants; they struck like predatory sea anemones, whipping through the air with a faint, crystalline whistle. Three pale tendrils punched directly into the torn fabric of Thorne’s woolen sweater. He didn't just scream; he arched his spine backward until his vertebrae popped against the bulkhead, his jaw unhinging as his lungs emptied in a high, whistling shriek. The tendrils didn't burrow into his flesh—they mineralized it on contact. Through the torn wool, Elena could see the flesh of his shoulder turning greyish-white, the capillaries freezing into dendritic networks of brittle calcium-phosphate crystals. The cylinder slipped from his numb fingers, crashing onto the deck with a hollow boom. The impact sheared the damaged locking ring completely off. The heavy steel lid bounced away, rolling down the center aisle of the lifeboat. From the open mouth of the canister, a dense, foaming mass of translucent biomass erupted, expanding into the warm, one-atmosphere cabin like expanding polyurethane insulation. Cerulean light flared violently within the foam. The lifeboat’s digital instrument panel instantly went haywire. The battery readouts flashed erratic hexadecimal codes, the cabin lights buzzed at an ear-splitting frequency, and the mechanical rudder indicators spun wildly on their spindles. Thorne dropped to his knees, his right arm already completely stiff, frozen in a crooked, calcified arc. The white frost was creeping up his neck, tracing along the carotid artery toward his jawline. His eyes rolled toward Elena, wide with absolute horror, his tongue struggling against teeth that were already knitting together with fibrous crystal. "...k-kill... it..." he choked out, the words muffled behind a hardening mask of white crust. Elena didn't hesitate. She grabbed the emergency fire axe mounted in its quick-release brackets beside the forward control console. The axe was forged high-carbon steel with a non-conductive rubber grip. She raised it high, stepping over the writhing pool of expanding biomass that was already beginning to anchor into the fiberglass floorboards. She didn't swing at Thorne. She swung the flat head of the axe straight into the emergency hatch release of the lifeboat's aft boarding door.
