The heavy tungsten-carbide rounds from the mercenaries’ submachine guns chewed into the mooring deck’s steel plating, throwing showers of white sparks against the howling gale. Elena did not run in a straight line. She ducked behind the massive drum of the automated mooring winch, using the half-inch braided steel hawsers as an improvised shield. Bullets whipped through the air with hollow, cracking snaps, one glancing off the cast-iron winch housing and singing past her cheek with the heat of a blowtorch. The mercenaries weren't firing with disciplined control anymore. Panic had dissolved their formation. Behind them, the deckhouse was disintegrating into a surreal forest of pale, crystalline calcification. Huge, translucent tendrils were punching straight through the steel bulkhead walls, wrapping around the navigation mast and hauling it downward into the maw of the collapsing hold. Elena reached the base of the free-fall lifeboat’s boarding platform. The survival craft was a sealed orange pod perched on a thirty-foot launch ramp tilted precipitously over the boiling froth of the ship's stern. Its heavy, inward-opening hatch hung ajar, swinging on its hinges with the brutal roll of the vessel. A mercenary broke through the mist ten paces behind her, his assault rifle raised. Before he could pull the trigger, the steel deck plating beneath his boots gave way. A geyser of freezing, hyper-saline sludge erupted upward, throwing the man into the air. A cluster of glowing cerulean filaments erupted from the void, snaking around his legs and chest, dragging him down into the torn hull plates before he could even draw a breath to scream. Elena hauled herself up the ladder of the ramp, her hands slipping on the frozen handrails. She hurled her body through the pod's oval hatch, falling onto the hard non-skid floor. The interior smelled of fiberglass resin, disinfectant, and stale emergency rations. Two rows of rear-facing bucket seats lined the cabin, each fitted with heavy four-point safety harnesses designed to absorb the crushing G-forces of a free-fall drop into open water. Through the narrow polycarbonate viewing port beside the hatch, Elena saw Dr. Aris Thorne reach the base of the ramp. His bridge coat was shredded, his face pale as chalk, but in his hands he clutched a sealed, stainless-steel cryo-containment canister—the core sample he had harvested from the lower hold. Behind him, the stern section of the Orion Trader was pitching steeply upward, lifting its twin bronze screws sixty feet out of the ocean as the fractured bow flooded. "Open the hatch!" Thorne screamed against the roar of the gale, pounding on the outer hull of the lifeboat with his fist. "Reyes! Open it! The data belongs to the consortium—it's worth billions!" Elena scrambled to the coxswain's console at the front of the craft. She looked at the heavy brass dogging wheel of the hatch. She could secure it and pull the release, leaving him to the abyss. Or she could let him board—along with the canister that held the dormant spark of the thing devouring the ship. Behind Thorne, rising from the shattered well of the engine casing, the towering bulk of the benthic mass breached the storm-tossed skyline. It wasn't just tendrils; it was an undulating, mountainous mantle of pale, pulsing flesh, crystalline nodes glowing with blinding violet and cerulean fire. It loomed over the stern, casting a shadow that eclipsed the lightning. Thorne lunged through the hatch, throwing the cryo-canister onto the floorboards with a heavy clang. He reached back to pull the door shut behind him. "Cut the release!" Thorne yelled, eyes wide with manic adrenaline. "Drop us! Now!" Elena didn't hesitate. She threw herself into the helm seat, snapped the primary harness buckle across her chest, and reached overhead for the primary launch handle. The mechanical trip was a heavy red lever marked: EMERGENCY FREE-FALL LAUNCH: PULL HARD DOWN. She gripped the textured steel, clamped her jaw tight, and yanked it down with all her strength. A pair of hydraulic pins retracted with a concussive bang. For half a second, the lifeboat rested weightless on the rails. Then gravity caught it. The orange capsule plunged down the greased steel ramp, accelerating into forty feet of open air before slamming into the freezing Atlantic trough.
