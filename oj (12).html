The hiss of cryogenic foam drowned out the storm. Pressurized nozzles mounted on the mercenaries' harnesses spewed thick, sub-zero slurry across the lower catwalks. The moment the white compound touched the bioluminescent growth, a violent thermal shockwave cracked through the engine room. The cerulean glow flashed blindingly bright before shattering into brittle, lifeless flakes of calcified dust. A high-pitched, harmonic whine—like pressurized steam venting through a microscopic fracture—screamed from the walls. The biomass was recoiling from the sudden plunge in temperature. On the ladder below Elena, the atmospheric diving suit shuddered. The cryogenic spray coated Dr. Sorenson’s titanium carapace in dense frost. The white tendrils coiling around the suit's hydraulic joints stiffened and snapped like dry twigs. The pincer slipped from the iron rungs with a dull clang, and the massive half-ton suit plummeted thirty feet into the flooded bilge, sending a wave of oily, freezing water crashing across the lower deck plates. Above her, the lead operator swung his rifle beam onto the middle catwalk. The glare locked onto Elena’s orange inspection suit. "Unidentified civilian on catwalk three," the point man shouted through his rebreather. "Visual confirmation: Reyes. She’s active." Through the chemical fog, a taller figure stepped forward onto the upper platform. He didn't wear tactical body armor; he wore an insulated bridge coat over heavy woolens, carrying a tablet cradled in a hardened shock-mount. Dr. Aris Thorne leaned over the railing, looking down at her with clinical detachment. "You're exceptionally resilient, Inspector Reyes," Thorne's voice echoed through the metal cavern, calm and unimpressed by the surrounding ruin. "Dropping the anchor and cutting the Sulzer was crude, but effective. You prevented a collision that would have complicated our extraction window." Elena pressed her back against the rusted bulkhead, her one bare wool sock slick against the steel grating. "You deployed biological weapons on a civilian shipping lane, Thorne. Twenty-two men are dead or fused to the bulkheads." "Not dead. Incorporated," Thorne corrected smoothly, his thumb tapping the tablet screen. "Their neural pathways are functioning as distributed processing nodes for the colony. The benthic biomass has spent four billion years in the abyssal silence of the Molloy Deep. It lacks specialized sensory organs for low-pressure environments. The human nervous system provides an ideal cognitive bridge to translate radio frequencies, electrical switches, and hull harmonics." He signaled with two fingers. The two flanking mercenaries raised their compact submachine guns, aiming non-lethal shock darts straight at her torso. "Secure her," Thorne said. "Then hook the liquid nitrogen manifolds to the main core in Hold 4. We drop the core into the transport cradle on the Aegir's deck before the gale tears this hull apart." "Doctor!" an operator by the auxiliary switchboard barked suddenly. "Look at the telemetry. The acoustic frequency isn't dying down." Thorne frowned, turning back to his tablet. The cerulean glow in the engine room was gone, snuffed out by the cryogenic chemical blanket. But the vibration hadn't stopped. In fact, it had shifted pitch, dropping below the threshold of human hearing into an infrasonic rumble that vibrated Elena's chest cavity like a bass drum. The puddle in the bilge void wasn't rippling from the storm anymore. It was boiling. Not with heat, but with cavitation bubbles. Tiny spheres of vacuum forming and collapsing under immense pressure waves surging up from the open sea through the torn double bottom. From the dark water beneath the lowest catwalk, a sound rose that made the mercenaries lower their weapons in sheer dread: a hollow, metallic groan that resonated along the entire length of the ship's 600-foot keel. The entity aboard the Orion Trader had not been calling out into the empty void. It had been signaling a parent mass. And something immense, rising from the floor of the North Atlantic trench, had just bumped against the freighter's keel.
