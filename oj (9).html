The stairwell had become a lung. As Elena descended past the bridge deck, the atmospheric pressure climbed unnaturally, popping her ears with every flight of metal steps. The air was warm, thick, and suffocatingly moist, carrying the coppery tang of heated wiring and the foul, brine-choked reek of decaying sea floor. White, crystalline filaments laced the bulkheads, pulsing with faint cerulean luminescence in time with the roaring engine below. Where the growths touched the steel deck plates, they had sweated out an alkaline slime that dissolved the non-skid paint into slick, grey sludge. She kept one hand pressed against the fire-hose rack for balance as the freighter rolled violently against the rising Atlantic swells. Every five seconds, the ship screamed—a shrieking mechanical shudder that traveled up the hull frames as the dragging anchor chain caught on undersea rock shelves, fighting the massive two-stroke diesel engine driving them forward at full throttle. Reaching the accommodation deck, she paused. The door to the mess hall had been crushed outward. Peering through the twisted steel frame, Elena saw what had become of the crew. They weren’t floating or butchered. They were integrated. Four bodies in denim and oilskins were fused together against the far bulkhead, caught in a massive web of pale, fibrous calcification that spanned the entire width of the mess. The tissue was hard as ivory in some places, soft and undulating in others, drawing nutrients and thermal energy directly from the men’s bodies and the severed electrical trunk lines overhead. Tiny, hair-like tendrils flickered along their necks and jawlines, conducting faint ripples of blue light across their skin. They weren't moving, but their chests rose and fell in a grotesque, synchronized rhythm. They were breathing with the ship. A sickening jolt hit Elena's stomach. She turned her eyes away and pressed forward, throwing her weight against the heavy fire door leading to the central engine casing. She stepped out onto the upper catwalk of the engine room. The space was a roaring iron cathedral. Six stories beneath her, the massive Sulzer diesel was hammering like an artillery battery, its six-foot-wide cylinder heads rocking in their mounts under maximum pressure. The ambient heat hit her like a physical blow—easily over a hundred and ten degrees. The emergency mechanical fuel cut-off was located four levels down, mounted on the forward bulkhead near the high-pressure fuel pumps. It was a simple steel lever, painted bright red, rigged via mechanical wire straight to the primary fuel injectors. Pulling it would starve the engine of heavy fuel oil within three seconds. Elena grabbed the handrails of the vertical steel ladder and began her descent. Halfway down the third platform, the ship took a violent, shuddering heel to port. Through the massive ventilation louvers on the forward bulkhead, a blinding beam of light pierced the oily haze. The Aegir was frantically sweeping the Orion Trader with high-intensity arc lamps, desperate to illuminate the rogue freighter bearing down on them. The collision alarm on the Aegir was audible even over the roar of the Sulzer—a shrill, rhythmic klaxon howling across the open water. On their current trajectory, impact was less than two minutes away. Elena reached the lower machinery flat. Her boots splashed into ankle-deep water; the double bottom was completely flooded, the bilges overflowing with freezing, inky seawater that boiled and hissed as it touched the burning-hot crankcase of the engine. She waded toward the fuel cut-off panel. Standing between her and the red lever was a dense, towering spire of white biomass that had erupted directly through the deck plates from the void beneath Hold 4. The spire was anchored to the fuel manifold, its pale, semi-translucent tendrils coiled tightly around the brass supply lines, drawing the heat of the fuel oil directly into its core. Embedded within the center of the spire was a salvage diver's atmospheric diving suit—a rigid titanium shell used for extreme ocean depths. Inside the reinforced acrylic dome of the helmet, a pair of clouded, milky eyes opened. The diving suit’s hydraulic arm twitched. The heavy pincer-grip rose, leveling straight at Elena.
