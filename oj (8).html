Elena lunged back into the wheelhouse, keeping her boots clear of the twitching mass of calcified sinew and slicker fabric that had been Chief Vance. The corpse was dragging itself toward the primary distribution board, fibrous tendrils lashing at the air, snapping and reforming like molten glass. She snatched the handset off the radio console. "Research vessel Aegir, this is Maritime Safety Inspector Reyes aboard the Orion Trader. The crew is gone. Containment in Hold 4 has suffered a catastrophic structural breach. The organism has compromised steering and primary electrical. I need immediate extraction!" A beat of hollow static hissed over the line, followed by the measured, clipped voice of a man who sounded neither surprised nor panicked. "Inspector Reyes. This is Dr. Aris Thorne, expedition director for Vanguard Maritime Sciences. You are trespassing on an active corporate salvage claim. Step away from the navigation console." "Salvage claim?" Elena’s grip tightened on the mic until her knuckles went white. "Your 'specimen' crushed the lower pipe tunnels, flooded the double bottom, and reanimated your chief engineer like a bio-electric relay. The ship is listing fifteen degrees and broadcasting a seismic pulse into the basin! What did you drag out of that trench?" A low chuckle cut through the transmission. "A baseline colonial organism adapted to three hundred atmospheres of hydrostatic pressure. At sea level, its cellular walls don't tear—they seek equilibrium by consuming available mass and converting ambient thermal energy. The Orion Trader was never meant to reach Halifax, Inspector. It was a mobile incubation platform." Elena’s blood ran cold. "You sacrificed twenty-two merchant mariners." "We gave them hazard pay they couldn't spend," Thorne replied flatly. "The Aegir is two nautical miles off your starboard quarter. We are matching your vector. Maintain vessel orientation until our recovery skiff secures the hull." Elena looked out through the rain-streaked windows of the bridge. Cutting through the dark swells of the squall was a sleek, grey-hulled research vessel, easily three hundred feet long. Its deck was bristling with heavy hydraulic winches, deep-sea submersibles in cradles, and high-powered arc lamps that turned the falling sleet into blinding ribbons of white. They weren't here to rescue anyone. They were here to reel the Orion Trader in like a catch, or scuttle her once they extracted whatever data had been generated during the breach. Behind her, the corpse of Vance pulled itself upright against the engine telegraph. The pale, chalky crust had spread over its entire head now, forming a featureless, domed carapace. The thing’s hand clamped down on the twin throttle levers, shoving both forward into the detent for MAXIMUM CONTINUOUS RATING. The deck roared. Below their feet, the massive Sulzer two-stroke diesel surged with an ear-splitting howl as fuel injectors flooded the cylinders. The freighter lurched forward, dragging its severed anchor chain through the seabed shelf with a grinding shriek that vibrated through every weld in the hull. The Orion Trader wasn't slowing down. It was accelerating—accelerating directly toward the approaching Aegir. On the radio, Thorne’s calm composure shattered. "Trader! Drop throttle! You are on a collision course—Trader, disengage the governors!" Elena dropped the mic. The bridge controls were completely unresponsive; the filaments snaking through the instrument panels had fused the throttle linkages into place with solid, calcified resin. There was only one way to stop the runaway vessel before it pulverized the research ship or snapped its own keel under the immense strain: she had to manually trip the emergency fuel shut-off valves in the engine room's lower casing. And to get there, she had to climb down through the superstructure—straight into the heart of the creeping white hive.
