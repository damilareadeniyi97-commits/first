The grip around Elena’s ankle was not human fingers. It was a dense braid of fibrous tendrils, cold as sea-ice and rigid as iron cable. The pull was sudden and merciless. Her feet swept out from under her, and she slammed onto her back in the flooded bilge. Freezing, oil-filmed seawater surged over her chest and neck, burning her nostrils with diesel fumes. The creature was dragging her backward toward the keel void—the flooded crawlspace where Hold 4’s breached floor met the double-bottom tanks. Elena twisted her torso, choking on the oily spray, and brought the steel pry bar down with both hands. The tapered point drove deep into the knot of cords wrapped around her boot. A thick, greyish fluid—viscous and smelling sharply of ammonia—erupted from the puncture. The tendrils spasmed, whipping like severed hydraulic hoses, but their grip did not fully release; instead, microscopic hair-like filaments burrowed into the synthetic rubber of her boot, trying to anchor into the fabric of her immersion suit. Above her, the titanium diver took a ponderous, crushing step forward. Deprived of the engine’s furnace-heat, the biomass infusing Dr. Sorenson’s suit was adapting, drawing instead on the ambient bio-electric potential of the ship's dying backup batteries. Cerulean light arced across the titanium joints, snapping like miniature lightning bolts across the wet deck plates. Elena planted her free foot against the edge of a steel floor grating, kicked violently against the pry bar to widen the tear, and ripped her foot free. The boot stayed behind, swallowed instantly into the churning dark water of the bilge void. She scrambled up the steel vertical ladder, climbing with one booted foot and one heavy wool-socked foot that slipped against the greasy rungs. Behind her, the diver’s titanium arm swung upward. The pincer clamped onto the bottom rungs of the ladder, groaning under mechanical strain as the creature began to climb. Elena didn't look down. She reached the third platform catwalk, gasping for breath in the sweltering, stagnant air. With the main engine dead, the ship’s electrical grid was completely destabilized. The emergency generators in the upper deckhouse tried to engage, sputtering with a frantic whine, but the filaments threaded through the main distribution switchboard shorted the circuits. Across the vast cavern of the engine room, the overhead mercury-vapor lamps exploded in a sequential chain of orange sparks. Total darkness slammed down. The only illumination left came from the creature itself. The biomass encrusting the walls, pipes, and walkways glowed with an eerie, rhythmic cerulean pulse—a slow, breathing cadence that bathed the steel cathedral in ghostly ocean light. Then, from the machinery casing above, came the hiss of pressurized gas. Elena froze against the catwalk railing. Small, metallic canisters were dropping through the overhead ventilation shafts, bouncing off the steel grating with hollow clatters. They tumbled through the glowing dark and rolled onto the platforms. Psssssssshh. Plumes of dense, yellow-white aerosol sprayed violently into the room. Elena recognized the acrid, peppery sting instantly: CS tear gas mixed with halogenated aerosol fire retardant—a commercial anti-riot and boarding suppression agent. High above, on the upper intake catwalk, figures in matte-black tactical gear and full-face rebreathers were descending via fast ropes dropped from the open deck. The beam of their rifle-mounted tac-lights cut through the chemical haze like white scalpel blades. Vanguard’s security team from the Aegir had boarded the freighter. A laser sight painted a crimson dot squarely over the acrylic visor of the titanium diver on the ladder below. "Target acquired," a voice barked through a respirator amplifier from the upper catwalk. "Module 36 has colonized host equipment. Do not damage the central cluster. Use cryogenic suppression foam—keep the core viable!" They weren't here to neutralize the threat. They were here to harvest it. And Elena was trapped on the catwalk right between the hunters and their prey.
