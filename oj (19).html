The cabin of the CH-148 Cyclone smelled of kerosene, warm hydraulic fluid, and wool. A flight medic wrapped Elena in three layers of thermal fleece, forcing a foil pack of self-heating broth into her shaking hands. Across the bay, the crew chief secured the hoist collar, sliding the heavy side door shut against the screaming gale outside. Through the circular observation porthole, the Atlantic vanished into an unbroken sheet of grey fog. "Core temperature is thirty-four point two," the medic said, shouting into his headset microphone. "Severe local frostbite on the left foot, stage-one hypothermia, but vitals are stable. We’re forty minutes out from CFB Shearwater." Elena leaned back against the nylon webbed seating, closing her eyes. The rhythmic thump-thump-thump of the twin turboshaft engines was mechanical, predictable, and clean. It lacked the grotesque, biological vibration that had rattled her teeth inside the Orion Trader’s hull. When the helicopter touched down on the tarmac at Shearwater, the storm had chased them inland. Rain swept sideways across the runway under the sodium-vapor security towers. Two men in dark, weather-proof parkas waited at the base of the hangar apron alongside three base security officers. Neither wore military insignia. Elena was helped down the ramp into a wheelchair by the medic, her left foot wrapped in thick medical gauze. The taller of the two men stepped forward, displaying a laminated federal credential. "Inspector Elena Reyes?" His tone was clipped, formal. "I’m Special Agent Miller, Transport Canada Marine Security Taskforce. We’ve been tracking the Orion Trader’s loss-of-signal since Tuesday. We’re taking over primary debrief." Elena looked at him, noting the subtle tension in his posture, the quick glance he exchanged with his partner when his eyes scanned her bare hands. "Where is the Coast Guard liaison?" "Classified under maritime security protocol," Miller replied smoothly. He gestured to two orderlies waiting with an insulated stretcher. "We need to get you into decontamination before general processing. Standard quarantine for unflagged biological hazmat." "There is no biological hazmat on me," Elena said. "The specimen dissolved upon contact with freezing surface air." Miller’s eyes narrowed slightly. "We’ll verify that through standard medical screening, Inspector. The vessel's manifests—along with any physical records or salvage documentation recovered from the bridge—must be surrendered immediately to federal custody." He was looking for the drive. Dr. Thorne had said Vanguard had backers—entities willing to let twenty-two merchant sailors die to test a living deep-sea relic. A corporate outfit with those kinds of resources didn't leave government docks unmonitored. Elena pulled her hands beneath the thermal blanket, her fingers resting lightly over the inner lining of her waterproof vest. The thumb drive remained safely tucked behind the heavy ballistic nylon of her personal knife sheath—a spot no standard medical search would inventory until she was logged into a civilian trauma ward. "The logbooks went down with the bridge," she lied, her voice hoarse and raw. "The whole ship broke its back and dragged itself into the trench. You can verify it on satellite." Miller stared at her for a long, calculating second. The wind whipped rain between them, rattling the hangar’s corrugated siding. "We'll review your formal statement once the doctors clear you," Miller said quietly. He signaled to the orderlies. "Wheel her in."
