The impact lifted the Orion Trader four vertical feet out of the water. Six hundred feet of structural steel screamed under vertical shear. Deck plates warped, popping rows of heavy rivets that ricocheted through the engine room like bullets. On the upper catwalk, two mercenaries lost their footing, their boots skittering across the oily steel before they crashed hard against the safety chains. Thorne was thrown against the distribution console, his tablet slipping from his grip and shattering on the grating below. The ship settled back down with a sickening, sluggish wallow. It didn't bounce on the swell; it was being held. Something colossal was draped across the freighter’s keel, an abyssal mass displacing tens of thousands of tons of deep water. The infrasonic thrum intensified, vibrating at a frequency so dense it turned the chemical fog into shimmering patterns of standing waves. In the bilge below, the black seawater receded—not draining, but displaced by an encroaching wall of pale, translucent matter rising through the sheared double-bottom framing. It wasn't branching or fibrous like the small colony from Hold 4. It was vast, ancient, and layered like compressed shale, pulsing with blinding bands of deep ultraviolet luminescence. "Hold your fire!" Thorne scrambled upright, blood trickling from a cut above his brow. His clinical detachment was completely gone, replaced by a frantic, breathless hysteria. "It's the benthic matriarch! It tracked the acoustic relay from the continental shelf! Rig the heavy cables—we have to take a tissue core before—" "Doctor, the hull is buckling!" the security lead shouted, hauling himself up by a conduit pipe. "The keel is snapping midships! We need to evacuate to the Aegir now!" "Nobody leaves until we log the resonant frequency!" Thorne lunged toward the communication line. Elena didn't wait to see who won the argument. While the mercenaries were disoriented and scrambling for handholds, she bolted. She darted across the third-tier catwalk, taking the companionway up toward the steering flat. Her bare, wool-socked foot was numb, throbbing with sharp, freezing pain against the icy steel, but adrenaline blocked out the cold. She scrambled up two flights of vertical ladder, throwing her shoulder against the emergency escape hatch leading to the aft mooring deck. She hit the cold Atlantic air like a diver breaking the surface. The storm had evolved into a full-scale tempest. Forty-foot rollers hammered the ship's quarter, sweeping sheets of spray across the winches and bitts. Looking over the starboard quarter, Elena saw the Aegir. The high-tech research vessel was in catastrophic trouble. Drawn in close by Thorne’s orders to secure the freighter, the Aegir was now caught in the gravitational vortex created by the rising benthic organism. Massive, translucent tendrils—thick as redwood trunks—had breached the surface between the two ships, wrapping around the research vessel’s sleek hull, dragging its bow downward into the churning sea. Arc lamps flashed wildly against the black water as the Aegir’s crew fired flare guns into the mass, the red phosphorus burning uselessly against the frigid, rubbery hide. Beneath Elena, the Orion Trader groaned one final, terminal lament. A sharp, deafening crack—like thunder breaking directly overhead—split the ship. Right beneath Hold 4, the keel fractured. The bow and stern began to tilt independently, pivoting inward as the freighter broke in two. Down on the mooring deck, mounted to the transom, was the vessel's last resort: an enclosed, bright-orange free-fall lifeboat, angled downward on a forty-five-degree steel ramp directly over the churning wake. Elena limped frantically toward the survival craft. Behind her, the hatch she had just exited blew off its dogs. A blast of freezing, pressurized mist erupted into the squall, and through the chemical vapor came the mercenaries, firing wild suppression bursts into the shadows as the superstructure collapsed behind them.
