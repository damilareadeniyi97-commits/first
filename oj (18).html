The amber beacon on the roof of the survival craft flashed into the driving squall, painting rhythmic arcs of gold across the white foam of the cresting waves. Inside, the cold was absolute. Elena pulled an aluminum space blanket from the first-aid kit mounted beside the helm, wrapping it tightly over her soaked immersion suit. Her left foot was completely numb, the wool sock stiffened with salt brine. Every fifteen seconds, a heavy Atlantic roller picked up the six-ton fiberglass pod and tossed it violently into the trough, the hull groaning against the crushing hydraulic slap of the sea. She forced herself to sit in the coxswain's bucket seat, staring through the narrow polycarbonate window. The Orion Trader was gone. Not a scrap of wreckage remained on the surface. No containers bobbed in the slick; no life rafts; no oil sheen. The sheer downward suction of the benthic mass had dragged all six hundred feet of iron and cargo straight into the abyssal depths, leaving behind only an unnatural patch of glass-flat water in the middle of a screaming gale. Elena reached into the interior pocket of her immersion suit. Her trembling, frost-bitten fingers closed around the plastic shell of the USB drive she had ripped from the staging terminal in the lower machinery space. It was still intact. The encrypted logs. The audio transcripts between Thorne and Vance. The acoustic telemetry from the Molloy Deep expedition. The incontrovertible proof that Vanguard Bio-Geophysical Consortium hadn’t accidentally stumbled upon a deep-sea hazard—they had systematically harvested it, knowingly risking commercial shipping corridors and sacrificing merchant crews to test the viability of a living weapon. A sharp, low-frequency hum rattled the small compass housing above the helm. Elena stiffened. She leaned forward, pressing her forehead against the freezing glass. Deep beneath the survival craft, down in the pitch-black water where the light of the storm could not reach, a faint cerulean glow was rising. It wasn't moving toward the lifeboat. It was descending. A colossal constellation of bioluminescent nodes, spreading across miles of ocean trench, slowly sinking back toward the ocean floor. The matriarch was retreating to the abyss, taking the iron corpse of the freighter with it, sealing the rift under six thousand meters of crushing hydrostatic pressure. The acoustic hum faded until the only sound left was the howling wind and the monotonous click-whirr of the analog compass resetting itself to magnetic north. Two hours crawled past in freezing silence. Then, slicing through the driving sleet from the western quadrant, a pair of twin halogen searchlights swept across the water. A heavy, dual-rotor roar cut through the gale—the unmistakable thunder of a Canadian Armed Forces CH-148 Cyclone maritime helicopter. The aircraft banked hard, its downwash flattening the chop around the orange capsule into a spray of white mist. The radio speaker in the lifeboat crackled with static, powered by the dying analog battery: "Survival craft drifting off sector four-bravo, this is Rescue 901. We have your emergency beacon locked. Stand by for hoist deployment." Elena let out a long, ragged breath, watching her condensation bloom on the glass. She gripped the thumb drive in her fist, slid it deep into her inner tactical seam, and pressed the lever to crack open the upper retrieval hatch.
