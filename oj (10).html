The heavy titanium pincer snapped shut with the crack of a pistol shot, missing Elena’s shoulder by inches. The displacement of air smelled intensely of scorched machine oil and deep-trench brine. Inside the atmospheric diving suit, Dr. Sorenson—or whatever biological substrate remained of him—didn't move with Vance's clunky, puppet-like hesitation. The suit’s pneumatic joints hissed, powered by high-pressure hydraulic lines that had been integrated directly into the organism’s pulsing, fibrous vascular system. Thick white cords of tissue coiled like braided steel wire through every joint, flexing with terrifying mechanical advantage. Elena lunged backward into the knee-deep oily bilge water, her boots slipping on the slick diamond tread. The titanium figure advanced, dragging the massive spire of biomass behind it like an anchor of living coral. The creature wasn't just defending itself; it was shielding the fuel lines. It fed directly on the superheated heavy fuel oil coursing through the manifold, using the thermal energy to accelerate its cellular expansion across the ship's lower frame. Above them, the ship’s hull plates groaned under an agonizing lateral twist. Through the ventilation gratings, the blinding glare of the Aegir’s searchlights flared across the oily mist. Over the roar of the Sulzer engine, Elena could hear the shriek of the Aegir’s bow thrusters churning at emergency power, trying desperately to back down from the oncoming collision course. Sixty seconds to impact. If the Orion Trader rammed the research vessel at full throttle, both hulls would buckle, spilling thousands of tons of burning bunker fuel into the freezing Atlantic—and setting the benthic entity free in the open sea. The red mechanical fuel shut-off lever sat ten feet behind the armored diver, half-submerged in a tangled knot of calcified tendrils. Elena scrambled to her feet, backing toward the auxiliary tool rack mounted along the engine casing. Her gloved hand found the grip of a three-foot steel pry bar chained to the bulkhead. With a hard wrench, she snapped the retaining bracket, freeing the heavy iron rod. The diver lunged. The heavy pincer swung in a vicious horizontal sweep meant to crush her ribcage. Elena didn't try to parry the titanium arm. She dropped flat into the freezing bilge water. The pincer struck the heavy steel casing of the high-pressure fuel pump with a ringing clang, shattering the pressure gauge and severing a high-pressure brass return line. A pressurized mist of diesel fuel erupted into the air, igniting against an exposed electrical conduit with a savage burst of orange flame. The flash illuminated the faceplate of the helmet. Inside, Sorenson’s jaw was dropped in a permanent, silent scream, thick dendritic filaments blooming through the eye sockets, pressed hard against the inner curve of the acrylic dome. The heat from the flare made the creature recoil. The pale tendrils along the suit’s joints contracted violently, hissing as the fire singed their semi-translucent skin. It fears thermal spikes. It could metabolize steady engine heat, but open flame threatened the delicate hydration boundary keeping its surface cells stable. Elena didn't hesitate. She scrambled through the burning pool of diesel, ignoring the searing pain against her immersion suit, and shoved the tapered end of the pry bar straight through the tangle of white tissue choking the red emergency lever. She used the bar as a class-one lever, bracing it against the engine frame and putting every ounce of her body weight into the pry. "Trip, damn you!" she screamed. The calcified tendrils snapped with the sound of snapping bone. The rusted mechanical lever slammed down into the EMERGENCY FUEL CUTOFF: TRIPPED detent. The effect was instantaneous. The mechanical trip slammed the high-pressure fuel racks shut. Starved of diesel, the massive Sulzer engine misfired once—a concussive backfire that blew black soot through the exhaust louvers—and then seized. The deafening iron roar that had shaken the freighter for hours died away into a hollow, clattering whine as the massive flywheel slowly ground to a halt. Silence crashed down over the engine room, broken only by the hiss of steam, the drip of water, and the howling gale outside. Without engine thrust to fight the hanging anchor chain, the Orion Trader yawed violently to port, sliding sideways into the trough of a massive swell. Through the forward vents, the blinding arc lamps of the Aegir scraped past the Orion Trader’s starboard rail by a mere thirty feet. The research vessel’s wake hit the freighter like a sledgehammer, rocking the dead ship onto its side. The titanium diver stumbled, off-balance. The sudden loss of engine heat caused the biomass around the fuel lines to spasm, withering slightly as the supply of hot oil ceased. The helmet’s acrylic dome turned toward Elena. The white tendrils inside pulsed with an agitated, blinding burst of bioluminescent azure light. Then, from the darkness of the flooded bilge beneath her feet, something cold, wet, and solid clamped around Elena’s ankle.
